## The Story of King Midas and the Golden Touch
In ancient Greek mythology, King Midas of Phrygia was a wealthy and powerful ruler, but his greatest obsession was gold. He valued his crown, his treasures, and even his daughter more than anything else. One day, Dionysus, the god of wine and revelry, visited his kingdom. Along the way, his companion Silenus became lost and was found by Midas in the palace’s famous rose gardens.

Midas welcomed Silenus warmly, recognizing him as a former teacher of Dionysus. Grateful, Dionysus offered Midas any wish he desired. Midas, consumed by his love for gold, asked that everything he touched turn into pure gold. Dionysus warned him to think carefully, but Midas was eager to grant the wish.

The next day, Midas tested his power. He touched a table, a chair, his bed, and even the flowers in his garden — all instantly transformed into gold. At first, he was delighted, but soon his joy turned to horror. Bread, water, wine, and even his beloved daughter turned to gold when he touched them. He could no longer eat, drink, or hold his family.

Realizing his curse, Midas begged Dionysus to undo it. The god instructed him to wash his hands in the Pactolus River in Anatolia. After doing so, the golden touch was removed, and Midas was restored to normal.
